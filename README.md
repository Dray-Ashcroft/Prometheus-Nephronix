## 📖 About

An interactive 3D simulator of the human excretory system, from the whole body down to a single nephron. Students can:

- **follow the path urine takes** through the male and female body
- **look inside the kidney** and trace its blood supply and drainage
- **watch urine form**, step by step, as colour-coded particles move through the nephron
- **change body fluid or blood pressure** and see how ADH, renin–angiotensin and ANF respond

The content follows NCERT Class 11, *Excretory Products and Their Elimination*. Built for classroom smart panels; it works on laptops and phones too.

## 🧭 Four Stages

Four numbered buttons at the top move between stages.

| Stage | Title | What you see |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| **1** | **Excretory system** | Male and female bodies side by side. Hold **♂** or **♀** for 5 seconds to explore one, then follow the path urine takes: kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra. Turn on **Skeleton** to see the vertebral levels of the kidneys. |
| **2** | **Kidney L.S.** | A longitudinal section of the kidney with numbered dots: capsule, cortex, medullary pyramid, renal column, papilla, calyces, renal pelvis, hilum, ureter, renal artery and vein. |
| **3** | **Vessels & drainage** | Two routes through the kidney: blood in through the renal artery and out through the renal vein, and urine out from the pyramids through the calyces, renal pelvis and ureter. The animated **Flow** layer shows blood moving through the vessels down to the interlobar level. |
| **4** | **Nephron & urine** | A 3D juxtamedullary nephron with its blood vessels, in three tabs: **Structure**, **Urine formation** and **Regulation**. |

In every stage, tap a part or a numbered dot to read about it.

## 🧪 Urine Formation Tour

In Stage 4, the **Urine formation** tab runs a seven-step tour in NCERT wording. Colour-coded particles (water, Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻, glucose, urea and blood cells) move through the nephron as each step is explained.

| Step | Part | What happens |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | Bowman's capsule | **Glomerular filtration**: blood pressure filters the blood. Proteins and blood cells are too large to pass. |
| 2 | PCT | **Most reabsorption**: nearly all glucose and amino acids, and most electrolytes and water |
| 3 | Descending limb | **Water out** into the hypertonic medulla |
| 4 | Ascending limb | **Salts out**, leaving the filtrate dilute |
| 5 | Henle's loop and vasa recta | **Counter-current mechanism** builds the gradient from 300 to about 1200 mOsmol L⁻¹ |
| 6 | DCT | **Fine adjustment** of Na⁺, water, H⁺ and K⁺ |
| 7 | Collecting duct | **Concentrating the urine** as water is drawn out |

In the **Structure** tab, tap the magnified PCT cells to see **reabsorption across the brush-border epithelium**.

## 🎚️ Regulation

The **Regulation** tab has two sliders. Move them and watch the particles, hormone levels and urine change.

| Sub-tab | Slider | What it shows |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **ADH** | **Body fluid**, from fluid loss to excess water | Osmoreceptors, ADH release, water reabsorbed in the DCT and collecting duct, urine volume and concentration, and diabetes insipidus when ADH is almost absent |
| **Renin–angiotensin & ANF** | **Glomerular blood pressure / blood flow** | JG cells and renin, angiotensin II, aldosterone, and ANF acting as a check, with the effect on GFR and Na⁺ reabsorption |

## 🔬 About the Model

- The reabsorption and secretion rates for each segment are **illustrative numbers** that follow NCERT's description, not measured values.
- The male and female urethras are drawn by the simulator and are not part of the source models.
- The female skeleton is the male skeleton reshaped by the simulator, with a wider, shallower pelvis. Fine details of the pelvis are approximate.
- In the Regulation tab, the JGA marker shows only the JG cells in the afferent arteriole wall, because the DCT runs elsewhere in this model.
